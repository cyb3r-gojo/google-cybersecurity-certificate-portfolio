# Healthcare Clinic Ransomware Incident – Incident Handler’s Journal

This repository documents a real-world style ransomware incident affecting a small U.S. healthcare clinic. The case study captures the initial response, incident details, and first journal entry using the Incident Handler’s Journal template.

## Incident Overview

On a Tuesday morning at approximately 9:00 a.m., a small U.S. healthcare clinic experienced a major security incident that shut down daily operations. Several employees reported being unable to access medical records or essential files. Shortly afterward, ransom notes appeared on workstations stating that all company files had been encrypted by an organized cybercriminal group known for targeting healthcare and transportation sectors.

The attackers demanded a large ransom in exchange for the decryption key.
Initial investigation revealed the attack was triggered by targeted phishing emails carrying malicious attachments. When employees downloaded the attachment, malware executed and deployed ransomware, encrypting critical patient data and disrupting all digital operations.

### The 5 W’s of the Incident
**Who caused the incident?**

An organized cybercriminal group known for conducting ransomware attacks on healthcare and transportation industries.

**What happened?**

Employees received targeted phishing emails with a malicious attachment. Once opened, the malware executed ransomware, encrypting critical files and displaying ransom demands.

**When did it occur?**

Tuesday at approximately 9:00 a.m.

**Where did it happen?**

Across the healthcare clinic’s internal network and employee workstations.

**Why did it happen?**

Employees were tricked into opening a malicious attachment, granting attackers access to deploy ransomware and extort payment.

### Additional Notes

- Need to determine how many employees interacted with the phishing email.

- Email filtering, spam protection, and attachment scanning processes must be reviewed.

- Immediate containment steps should include isolating infected devices and taking systems offline.

- Validation of backups is critical to determine recovery options without paying the ransom.

### Key Learning Outcomes

This case study demonstrates:

- How phishing can lead directly to ransomware deployment

- The importance of secure email gateways and employee training

- The value of maintaining clean, offline backups

- The need for structured incident documentation through tools like the Incident Handler’s Journal

- How swiftly ransomware can disrupt healthcare operations and patient services
